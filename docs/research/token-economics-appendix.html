Research Appendix

Token Economics & How to Measure Value

The enterprise view: what AI actually costs, how value is measured, and why the unit of measurement decides whether you can see what you are buying.

Companion to the article "The Token Is Not the Bill."
Multi-agent deep research · 24 sources · 23 of 25 claims adversarially verified · June 13, 2026

How to read this appendix. Every claim carries its verification vote (e.g., 3-0 = unanimous across three independent skeptics) and a confidence rating. Source-strength caveats and the two refuted claims are recorded at the end. Pricing figures are verified as of June 2026 and will date quickly.

1. Headline thesis

Enterprises are caught between two opposing curves. Per-unit inference cost is collapsing — roughly 5–10× per year to reach a given capability (Epoch AI) — while total AI spend is rising, because cheap tokens induce far more token-hungry agentic workflows (a Jevons-paradox dynamic). Meanwhile the value side remains weak and hard to prove: only 39% of organizations attribute any EBIT impact to AI, most under 5% (McKinsey); Gartner forecasts 40%+ of agentic AI projects canceled by end of 2027; and a controlled trial found experienced developers 19% slower with early-2025 AI tools while believing they were 20% faster (METR). The through-line: cost-per-token is the wrong unit. Value is measured per business outcome, and the organizations capturing it redesign workflows rather than bolt AI onto existing ones.

2. Token-economics primer

Unit cost is falling fast — 5–10× per year (3-0, high)

Epoch AI: "very roughly a 5–10× cost reduction per year for reaching a given capability level." Reaching ~27% on the FrontierMath benchmark took ~43M output tokens in April 2025 (o4-mini) vs ~5M in December 2025 (GPT-5.2) — a ~3× drop in eight months. a16z's "LLMflation" analysis estimates ~10×/year. Epoch cautions the trend "might slow."

Output costs 3–5× input — output volume is the driver (3-0 / 2-1, high)

Output tokens are priced at a premium over input (a 3–5× lower bound). Anthropic is exactly 5× per tier — Opus 4.8 $5 / $25, Sonnet 4.6 $3 / $15, Haiku 4.5 $1 / $5 per million in/out; OpenAI GPT-4o ~4×; Google Gemini ~8×. What the model writes, not what you feed it, dominates most budgets.

Caching is the primary lever — 90% off reads (3-0, high)

On Anthropic, cache reads cost 0.1× base input (a 90% discount); cache writes carry a premium (1.25× for a 5-minute TTL, 2× for 1-hour). Break-even arrives on the first re-read. Caching economics are provider-specific — OpenAI and Google differ.

Inference is 80–90% of production spend (3-0, high)

FinOps Foundation: inference "can account for 80–90% of the total spend in many cases," corroborated by NVIDIA's ~80/20 inference/training framing. Optimization effort belongs on inference, not training.

Falling unit cost ≠ falling total spend (Jevons) (2-1, medium)

Cheaper inference pushes firms toward more compute-intensive agentic architectures, making aggregate demand "super-elastic" — total spend rises as cost-per-token falls (arXiv 2601.12339). Corroborated: Deloitte (Jan 2026) reports cost-per-token down ~10× in two years while consumption rose ~100×; agentic coding can use ~1,000× the tokens of equivalent chat. The "super-elastic / structural Jevons paradox" framing is from a single non-peer-reviewed preprint; the empirical core is multiply corroborated.

3. The ROI evidence — bullish and skeptical

Skeptical (well-sourced)

  • McKinsey State of AI (3-0, high) — only 39% attribute any EBIT impact to AI; most of those say under 5%; only ~6% are "AI high performers."
  • Gartner, 2025-06-25 (3-0, high; analyst forecast) — 40%+ of agentic AI projects canceled by end of 2027 (escalating cost, unclear value, inadequate risk controls); "most agentic AI propositions lack significant value or ROI." Survey of 3,400+ organizations.
  • METR randomized controlled trial, 2025 (3-0, high) — early-2025 AI tools made experienced developers 19% slower on real issues; they forecast a 24% speedup and still believed in a 20% speedup afterward. n=16 expert devs on mature codebases with early-2025 tooling — do not generalize to juniors, greenfield, or 2026 tools.

Bullish (weaker sourcing)

A Microsoft-sponsored IDC report claims large GenAI ROI multiples; treat vendor-sponsored multiples with caution (sponsorship incentive, self-reported). The widely repeated MIT/NANDA "95% of GenAI pilots fail" statistic did not survive into the verified set — see open questions.

4. How enterprises measure value

FinOps for AI — the emerging standard (3-0, high)

Defines AI-specific KPIs — Cost Per Inference = total inference cost / requests; Cost Per Token = total cost / tokens — treats cost-per-token as a new core usage metric (volatile pricing, GPU scarcity), and frames ROI around six business-value priorities: cost efficiency, resilience, user experience, productivity, sustainability, business growth.

Measure the use case, not the token (3-0, high)

FinOps Foundation: "the only real way to understand the TCO of leveraging AI for a business outcome is to measure the entire use case" — across models, infra, integration, human oversight, and governance. Tokens are "only a small piece of the puzzle." The unit that matters is cost per useful business outcome, and human verification — absent from the API invoice — is often the most expensive component.

5. Why most fail — and what the winners do

The winners redesign workflows (3-0, high)

McKinsey: 80% set efficiency as the AI objective, but value-capturers "often set growth or innovation as additional objectives... most are redesigning workflows." Of 25 attributes tested, workflow redesign has the single highest correlation with EBIT impact; high performers are 2.8× more likely to report fundamental redesign. Correlational survey evidence, not causal proof.

Cost-control levers that work

  • Model routing / right-sizing (3-0, high) — routing to a non-reasoning model when reasoning isn't needed saves 4–20× tokens; organizations report 40–70% inference savings (RouteLLM: 35–85% while retaining ~95% of GPT-4 performance). Workload-dependent.
  • Prompt caching — the 90% read discount, for any re-read context.
  • Inference focus — since inference is 80–90% of spend, that's where optimization compounds.

6. Watch-list — open questions & weak-evidence flags

  1. The MIT/NANDA "95% of pilots fail" statistic did not appear among verified claims — a candidate thin-source figure. Trace the primary methodology before citing it as fact.
  2. Does the METR slowdown hold for 2026 agentic tooling, juniors, or greenfield work? The result is narrow.
  3. Durability of the 5–10×/year cost decline — Epoch itself cautions it "might slow"; GPU scarcity and benchmark contamination complicate the curve.
  4. Audited, third-party use-case-level ROI case studies (cost per business outcome) beyond vendor and survey self-reporting remain scarce.

Refuted in verification (do not use): "Context Window Creep is the largest hidden cost" (1-2); "Three-way cost/performance/impact balancing yields 30–50% better outcomes" (0-3).
